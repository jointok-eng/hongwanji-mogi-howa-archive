# The Life That Connects Us: Memory, Family, and Gratitude

*A Brief Talk on Buddhism — Honpa Hongwanji Mogi das Cruzes*

Welcome, everyone. Today we celebrate Gotan-e, the birth anniversary of our founder, Shinran Shonin. This month of May, as reflected by the deep devotion embodied in the memorial tablets (ihai) you hold today, is a sacred time to remember our departed loved ones. Shinran Shonin passed down to us the boundless compassion of Amida Buddha, who embraces all beings unconditionally. This infinite grace transcends the limits of time and pours over every existence equally.

Today, let us reflect on the "bonds of life." When does a person's existence truly vanish from this world? It is not with the physical death of the body, but rather when there is no longer anyone left to remember them—when they are completely forgotten. Then, when does an existence begin? It does not start simply at the moment of birth, but much earlier. It begins within the warm anticipation and sincere wishes for happiness held by those awaiting their arrival. Life ends when it is forgotten, and it begins when it is deeply loved and wished for.

Our lives expand far beyond the boundaries of time. With our present self living in the "now" as the center, our ancestors and our future descendants exist simultaneously. The central anchor that connects these strands of life, reaching from the past into the future—especially the living memories of our departed family members—is the family. Gathering here with these memorial tablets in hand is an enduring testament of love for the deceased and a sacred vow never to forget. We engrave their names and hold them close in our daily remembrance. Sharing their stories, memories, and wisdom within the family means passing down an invaluable spiritual treasure to the next generation. Maintaining this daily practice of remembrance inspires us to live each day with sincerity, care, and dedication.

The vital importance of this continuity is beautifully illustrated by the pioneers (Issei) who journeyed from Japan. In the face of immense hardships, they persevered with steady, diligent effort to lay the foundations of their lives. This daily consistency is the very foundation for all future growth. Among us today are those who inherit the bloodlines of these ancestors, as well as those who have come to deeply love Japanese cultural values. The earnestness, honesty, compassion, and gratitude shown by our pioneers are not merely outdated customs; they are critical "roots" that transcend nations and cultures, essential for a fulfilling life. Becoming aware of these roots and living our daily lives with sincerity, attentiveness, and gratitude enriches our community and illuminates our paths. Walking forward with stability is the highest expression of gratitude we can offer to those who have passed, bringing them true peace.

Shinran Shonin revealed to us that we are all unfailingly embraced by Amida Buddha. The light of Amida's compassion pours equally upon our ancestors, upon us who live in the present, and upon the future generations of children. To keep our departed loved ones alive in our hearts, to give thanks for our family lineage, and to walk forward into the future treasuring each day—within these actions lies a way of living that perfectly responds to the compassionate wish of Amida Buddha. When we recite the Nembutsu, "Namu Amida Butsu," we experience a profound connection with our ancestors and with Amida Buddha, filling our hearts with immense gratitude. May you carry the blessings of today's encounter in your hearts, maintaining your devotion to remember your loved ones and your determination to live each day with sincerity. This enduring continuity will sustain us and grant us the strength to make this land an even better place. Thank you very much for your presence today.
