# What Are We?

*A Brief Talk on Buddhism — Honpa Hongwanji Mogi das Cruzes*

What exactly are we? We experience waves of happiness and depths of sorrow, and although our emotional landscape shifts from moment to moment, our core sense of "self" endures. Our hearts have the boundless capacity to hold all these diverse emotions. This inner landscape, which feels uniquely personal, is actually a shared space that defines your true existence.

You represent a sacred crossroads where your ancestors—who have departed and live on through your memories—and your future descendants—who are yet unborn—meet in perfect unison. The past and the future, which can never physically coexist, intersect dynamically within you. Life is an unbroken thread passed down directly from your lineage, and it is through your existence that this vital thread will extend to the generations to come. You are never truly isolated; your very being is built upon the presence of others.

In Buddhism, we regard the sacred mindfulness beads, the Nenju, as a profound teaching tool. Consider the individual beads: each bead features a central hole, but that hole only gains its functional existence because it is surrounded and defined by the material of the bead itself. The beads touch, support, and sustain one another. You are very much like that central opening. Your form, character, and personality are continuously shaped by the individuals around you—your family, friends, and neighbors. They give your life its true context and meaning. Yet, it is the opening itself that allows the string to pass through, binding all the beads together in unity.

Of course, this human circle is always changing. Beloved companions depart from our sight, while new encounters arrive. Do those we lose simply vanish into nothingness forever? We naturally feel a heavy void, a quiet absence. What we must deeply realize, however, is that within the profound sorrow of loss, something vital remains completely untouched. It is not the physical person, but their love. The love of those who have passed before us continues to dwell within you, preserved in your warm memories. This love never expires; it remains anchored in your being, turning the empty void into a source of enduring inner warmth.

The Buddha teaches us that this remaining love transforms into an even more powerful spiritual force called compassion. Thus, grief is not an enemy to be feared; it becomes a sacred opportunity to awaken to this inner power. The deep empathy you experience through loss can be transformed into the very compassion you extend toward others. You can choose to make this active compassion a core part of who you are, spreading kindness to those around you. Transforming sorrow into genuine gratitude and gentleness is the true path. It is the most sublime way to honor those who have passed while simultaneously living a richer, more meaningful life. We do not exist in isolation; we are a vibrant web of connections. This enduring bond, which remains unbroken even through loss, is the saving wisdom that completes our lives.
